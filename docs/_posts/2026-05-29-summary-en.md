A DBOS blog post evaluates the viability and trade-offs of building durable workflow engines directly on Postgres, comparing with dedicated solutions like Temporal. Community comments highlight alternatives such as Armin Ronacher's absurd and River, while debating the limitations of DBOS's paid Conductor component. This discussion challenges the necessity of dedicated workflow engines like Temporal, potentially simplifying architecture for teams that already rely on Postgres. The interest reflects a broader trend toward using Postgres as a unified platform for both data and orchestration, though scalability and feature trade-offs remain. DBOS relies on a paid component called Conductor for scaling and recovery, which some critics see as a dealbreaker. **Background**: Durable workflows ensure that long-running processes can survive failures and continue from their last checkpoint, which is crucial for distributed systems. Temporal is a popular open-source dedicated workflow engine used by companies like Stripe and Netflix. Building such functionality directly on Postgres could reduce operational complexity by eliminating additional infrastructure, but raises questions about scalability, concurrency, and feature parity.
